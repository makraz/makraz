Software that carries a business rarely ends by breaking down. It slowly becomes unusable: three people know how to make it work, every other operation goes through an Excel export, and the smallest new business rule needs custom development nobody dares commission. At that point the tool no longer serves the company; the company organises itself around the tool's limits.

We build the other way round: the data model first, because that is what costs the most to correct, then the screens your teams will use every day. The goal is not the demo but the daily use — a tool where a new joiner works out what to do without three days of training, and where a changed business rule means a setting rather than a project.
