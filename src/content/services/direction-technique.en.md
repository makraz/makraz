title: Technical leadership & teams
lead: For when what you are missing is not developers, but someone accountable for the technology.
engagement:
  model: A short, scoped audit to establish where things stand, then a recurring part-time presence, or a dedicated team when the work also has to get built. No full-time hire, no equity dilution.
  drivers: The size and age of the codebase, how many people need leading, urgency — an incident in progress is not a fundraise preparation — and the length of the engagement.
faq:
  - q: When does a part-time CTO beat hiring one?
    a: "When technical decisions commit the company but do not yet fill a full-time role, or when you have to decide quickly: an executive hire takes months, an engagement starts within days."
  - q: Are you replacing our existing team?
    a: No. In the large majority of cases the team is capable and badly served by how it is organised. We look first for what is slowing it down, and only recommend changing people when the facts demand it.
  - q: What is left when the engagement ends?
    a: A documented architecture, written priorities, and if we hired for you, a team in place that does not need us. A successful engagement ends in your independence.

There comes a point where the problem is no longer the number of developers. Releases slip without anyone able to say why, every estimate is contradicted, nobody can answer "how long to add this?" clearly, and architecture decisions get made by default — under pressure, by whoever was available that day.

That is almost always a technical leadership vacuum, not a lack of skill. We take that role: settle the architecture questions, write the priorities down, say no when it is needed, hire the right profiles in the right order, and translate all of it into language your partners and investors can act on. You keep the decision; you stop making it alone.
