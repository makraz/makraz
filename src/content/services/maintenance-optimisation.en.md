An inherited application rarely arrives with a manual. It arrives with a departed developer, three-year-old dependencies, a deployment nobody dares run, and a verbally transmitted list of things "not to touch". The temptation at that point is to rewrite everything — the most expensive and riskiest decision available.

So we read first, then secure, before improving anything. In the large majority of cases inherited code is far more salvageable than it looks: what was missing was not quality, but someone taking the time to understand it and write down what they understood.
