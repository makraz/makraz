The question almost always arrives as a budget: "how much for a website?". That is the wrong way in. A brochure site and a custom platform are not two quality tiers of the same product; they are different objects, solving different problems and ageing in different ways.

## The real question: what changes, and who changes it?

A brochure site presents a settled business to visitors who leave with an impression and a way to get in touch. Its content changes a few times a year, and you are the one changing it.

A platform, by contrast, does someone's work. Users log in, enter data, trigger operations — orders, bookings, case files, payments. Its content changes constantly, driven by people who do not ask your permission first. That difference, not the page count or the polish of the design, decides what needs building.

## Three signs a brochure site is enough

You sell trust before the transaction: the client phones you, writes to you, or walks into your office. A practice, a tradesperson, a consultant all sit here — and an excellent brochure site returns more than an underused platform ever will.

Your offer fits on a few pages that barely move. And you have no internal process that needs to live in the site: quotes, files and follow-ups happen elsewhere, perfectly well, and have no reason to move.

## Three signs you need a platform

You already keep a shared spreadsheet that several people edit and nobody fully understands any more. That is almost always a business tool asking to exist.

Customers keep asking you for the same information and you retype it by hand. Or your revenue depends on a chain — stock, order, delivery, invoice — where every step is currently copied from one tool into another. Every retyping is an error waiting to happen.

One last test, more honest than the rest: if you cannot yet tell which one you need, start with the smallest object that answers the question. A well-built brochure site can grow a platform two years later; a platform built without users costs you immediately and serves nobody.
