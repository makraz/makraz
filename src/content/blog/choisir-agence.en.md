An MVP has to ship fast: that's its whole point. But 'fast' has become an excuse for 'badly'. The result: most MVPs that find their market must be rewritten within eighteen months — exactly when the business should be accelerating.

## Technical debt isn't a technical problem

It's a deferred cash-flow problem. Every shortcut taken today is a loan: you repay it later, with interest, as bugs, slowness and features that can't be added. A founder who understands this stops asking 'how long to go fast?' and starts asking 'what do we cut?'.

## Cut scope, never quality

The right way to go fast isn't writing dirty code: it's building fewer things. An MVP with three solid features beats a prototype with ten fragile ones. Scope is negotiable; architecture, tests on the business core and code cleanliness are not.

## Three rules we apply

1. Boring architecture: proven frameworks, no experiments in the foundation. 2. The business core tested from the first sprint — it must never break. 3. Continuous delivery: if deploying is scary, it is already too late.

The result: a product that ships in weeks, yet can still be built upon in year three. That's the whole difference between an MVP and a throwaway.
